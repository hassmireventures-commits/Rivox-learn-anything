What is an agentic AI tutor? How it differs from a chatbot

Published 11 October 2026

Ask a chatbot "explain photosynthesis" and you get a good paragraph. Ask it again tomorrow and it has no idea you asked, what you got wrong, or that your exam is in three weeks. That is not a flaw in the model. It is what a chat window is: a place that responds.

A tutor is different. A tutor remembers why you came, decides what you should do next, checks whether it worked, and changes course when it did not. "Agentic" is the word the industry now uses for software that does that kind of work between your messages instead of waiting for them.

Four things an agentic tutor does that a chatbot does not

1. It holds the goal

Your goal ("IELTS band 7 by March", "deadlift with good form", "ship a small Godot game") is the frame for everything. A module called "Advanced English" means something different inside an IELTS plan than inside a literature course. A tutor reads every task in the sense of your goal. A chatbot reads only the words in front of it.

2. It plans the next session

The hardest part of self-study is not the material. It is deciding what to do tonight. An agentic tutor picks one next step: the module you are on, the flashcards that are due, a short quiz on a weak topic. You open the app and start, instead of starting a negotiation with yourself.

3. It tests you, because recall is the lesson

Retrieval practice, pulling an answer out of memory instead of rereading it, is one of the best-replicated findings in learning research. Roediger and Karpicke's 2006 study is the classic reference: students who were tested on a passage remembered more a week later than students who reread it. A tutor builds that in. A chatbot waits for you to ask to be tested.

4. It corrects its own plan

Plans go wrong. A module is vague, a quiz drifts off topic, a step is too easy. A chatbot can apologise. An agentic tutor can rename the module, rebuild the quiz, or drop the step, and tell you what it changed.

What it should never do on its own

Autonomy is only good with limits. A sensible rule of thumb for any learning agent:

  • Do, then offer undo: small, reversible changes inside your plan, like renaming or reordering a module, opening the screen you asked for.
  • Ask first: anything that spends your money or AI quota, deletes your work, or sends you out of the app.
  • Never: claim it did something it did not, invent a link or a video, or keep nudging you after you turned nudges off.

If a tutor cannot tell you what it changed and how to reverse it, it is not agentic, it is unpredictable.

How to judge an AI tutor in five minutes

  1. Give it a goal and close the app. When you reopen it, does it know what to do next?
  2. Say a quiz was off topic. Does it fix the quiz, or tell you where to click?
  3. Answer a question wrong. Does that question come back later?
  4. Pick something unusual, like woodworking or watercolour. Does the practice fit the skill, or is it trivia?
  5. Ask what it changed this week. Can it say?

Questions people ask

What does agentic mean in an AI tutor?

The tutor acts toward your goal between your messages: it keeps the plan, decides the next session, tests you, and changes the plan when the evidence says it is wrong.

Should an AI tutor change my plan without asking?

Only for small, reversible changes it tells you about, with an undo. Anything that costs money, uses quota, deletes work, or leaves the app should wait for your tap.

Is an agentic AI tutor better than ChatGPT for studying?

For one explanation, a general assistant is excellent. For learning a subject over weeks, a tutor that remembers the plan and tests you usually does more. More on that in AI tutor app vs ChatGPT for studying.
