Local-First Flashcards: Spaced Repetition Without the Cloud

Published: September 7, 2026 · Updated: September 19, 2026

Spaced repetition is one of the most tested ideas in learning science. It is the engine behind Anki, SuperMemo, and most serious flashcard apps. The phrase gets used as a slogan, though. You still need to know what the schedule is doing, and why it matters where the deck lives.

The core idea: review right before you would forget

Memory does not fade at a constant rate. Right after you learn something, you forget it quickly. If a memory survives, it fades more slowly later. Spaced repetition tries to schedule the next review around the point you are about to forget. Too early wastes time. Too late means you relearn from scratch.

How the SM-2 algorithm actually works

The most widely used algorithm is SM-2 (SuperMemo 2), published in 1987. It is still the basis for a lot of software. Each card tracks three numbers:

  • Ease factor: how easy this card is for you. It often starts around 2.5 and moves up or down when you rate a review.
  • Interval: days until the next review. A new card often starts at 1 day, then jumps to about 6 days after a second success, then grows by roughly the ease factor.
  • Repetitions: how many times in a row you have gotten it right. One miss usually resets this and drops the interval back to 1 day.

Easy cards stretch out. Hard cards stay close. You do not have to manage the calendar by hand.

Why "local-first" matters here

A flashcard deck is a record of what you do not yet know, indexed by topic. That can be sensitive if you are prepping an exam, a certification, or an interview. Local-first means the cards, review history, and schedule live on the device by default, not on a server as a matter of course. You can still choose a backup. The default should not be "upload everything."

Where useful cards come from

A schedule is only as good as the deck. Two sources tend to work:

  • From mistakes: a quiz item you got wrong becomes a card. No extra generation needed. The deck matches your weak spots.
  • From notes you already have: a chapter, a handout, or a resume can become cards grounded in that text, not generic trivia on the topic name.

Avoid dumping a thousand AI cards you never rated. Ten honest cards from yesterday's misses beat a giant unread deck.

Frequently asked questions

Is SM-2 better than Anki's algorithm?

Anki's modern default is often FSRS, which uses a richer memory model. SM-2 is simpler and still a proven baseline. The real jump is going from no spaced schedule to any spaced schedule, not from SM-2 to a fancier variant.

Do I need the internet to review?

Not if the deck and schedule are stored locally. You only need a connection when you generate new cards from a cloud model.

What if I lose the phone?

Local-first means the data lives on that device unless you turned on a backup. Encrypted cloud backup is a good option if you want a recovery copy. Keep it off if you prefer the data never to leave the phone.
