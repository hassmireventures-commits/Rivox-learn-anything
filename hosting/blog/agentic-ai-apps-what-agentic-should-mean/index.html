Agentic AI apps: what "agentic" should mean for the person using them

Published 11 October 2026

A year ago the label was "AI-powered". Now it is "agentic". The word is doing real work in some products and none in others. From the user's side of the screen, here is a practical way to tell the difference.

The short definition

An agentic app's AI takes steps toward your goal, not only replies. It plans, uses the app's own tools (create, edit, schedule, search), checks the result, and corrects course. The important part is the last two words of that sentence people usually skip: within limits you can see.

Three tiers of autonomy

Good agentic apps sort every action into tiers and stick to them:

  1. Act and report, with undo. Cheap, reversible, inside your own space. Opening the screen you asked for. Renaming an item. Reordering a list. The app does it, tells you what it did, and gives you a one-tap undo.
  2. Propose and wait. Anything that spends money or quota, leaves the app, or produces something large. The app shows exactly what it will do and waits for a tap.
  3. Never alone. Deleting your work without a replacement, messaging other people, payments, anything you cannot take back.

If an app cannot tell you which tier an action is in, it has not thought about it.

Five signs of a real agentic app

  • It fixes, not just flags. When something is wrong, it changes the thing, instead of telling you which menu to open.
  • It is honest about what it did. It never says "done" for something it only suggested, and never invents a link, a file, or a result.
  • It keeps context between sessions. Your goal and the state of the work survive closing the app.
  • It grounds itself. Before writing about your topic, it checks it has the right sense of the words. A tutor that confuses "Advanced English" with a vintage car called the Advance has skipped this step.
  • It respects off switches. Notifications, nudges, and automatic actions each have a setting, and the setting wins.

Why this matters more for learning apps

A shopping agent that buys the wrong thing costs money once. A learning agent that drifts off topic costs hours, and you may not notice, because you do not yet know the subject well enough to see the drift. The best learning agents therefore check their own plans against your goal, and make their corrections visible so you can learn from them too.

Questions people ask

What is an agentic AI app?

An app whose AI takes steps toward your goal, plans, uses the app's own tools, checks results, and corrects course, within limits you can see.

Is it safe to let an AI app act on its own?

For small, reversible actions it reports with an undo, yes. Actions that spend money, delete data, message people, or leave the app should always need your confirmation.
