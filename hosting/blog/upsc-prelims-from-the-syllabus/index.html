UPSC prelims: read the syllabus before the news feed

Published 27 September 2026

The Civil Services exam is three stages. Prelims is the gate. Most people who stall do it in the same way: a daily pile of current affairs, and the official syllabus still unopened.

The notice for the year you sit is on upsc.gov.in. Counts, timings, and the qualifying mark belong in that PDF, not in a thumbnail. What follows is the shape that has held in recent cycles, and a way to practise against it. If the Commission changes a line, follow the notice.

Two papers, and only one of them ranks you

Prelims is two objective papers the same day. General Studies is the paper that decides the cutoff. The second paper, CSAT, is qualifying. You need the minimum the notice states. Scoring well above that minimum does not rescue a weak General Studies paper, and falling short of it ends the attempt even if General Studies was strong.

Recent notices deduct one third of the marks assigned to that question for a wrong answer. A blank is safer than a blind guess. Check the year’s rule before you build a guessing habit from an old test series.

CSAT still needs a plan if reading comprehension or basic numeracy is slow. It does not need a second full syllabus. A few timed passages and a few arithmetic sets a week are enough for many candidates. General Studies needs the actual list.

Static headings first

The General Studies syllabus is a short document and a long reading list: Indian polity and governance, history, geography, economy, environment, general science, and current events of national and international importance. The last item is the one that swallows the calendar if you let it.

Pick one heading for the week. Polity might be fundamental rights. Geography might be monsoon. Read one standard chapter, then ten previous-year questions that sit under that heading. The official previous papers are on the Commission’s site. A question you cannot place under a syllabus line is a sign you are collecting trivia.

News is useful when it has a heading

A bill, a report, or a place in the news belongs next to the syllabus line it touches. Write one sentence: what it is, and which heading it sits under. Twenty of those a month is a revision file. Two hours of scrolling with nothing written down is not.

Mains is a different task: essays and long answers, after you clear prelims. Practising a 1,000-word essay in month one, while the prelims syllabus is still a list you have not started, spends the only hours that move the gate. A short weekly note in your own words is enough to keep the writing muscle, without pretending you are already in the mains hall.

Other exams are other syllabi

NEET UG and JEE Main are school-science papers with a published question count. IELTS is four language papers. SSC and bank tests follow their own notifications. “Competitive exam GK” as a single topic produces questions that fit none of them.

Questions people ask

What are the two prelims papers?

General Studies, which decides the cutoff, and CSAT, which is qualifying. Both are objective, both are on the same day in the usual notice, and the year’s PDF on upsc.gov.in is the version that applies to you.

How does negative marking work?

Recent notices deduct one third of the marks for that question when the answer is wrong. Confirm it in the current notice. A blank is not the same penalty as a wrong tick.

Should I start with current affairs?

Start with a syllabus heading and the chapter under it. Attach news to that heading. A feed on its own is hard to revise and harder to turn into a question you can mark.
