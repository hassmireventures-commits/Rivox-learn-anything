Learn any skill with an AI study plan: fitness, DIY, art, game development, code

Published 11 October 2026

Most study apps were built for exams, so they test the same way whatever you learn: a fact, four options, one right answer. That works for the capital of Peru. It does very little for a squat, a dovetail joint, or a watercolour wash.

A good AI study plan changes how it practises to match how the skill is used. Here is what that looks like for five very different goals.

Fitness: cues, progression, safety

The useful questions are decisions you make under the bar. "Your knees cave in at the bottom of the squat. Which cue helps?" "You hit all your reps easily three sessions in a row. What changes next week?" The plan should space the technique work across the week, and always stay general: an app is not a doctor or a physiotherapist, and anything about pain or injury belongs with one.

DIY and making: tool, order, safety step

Practise the sequence and the choice: which drill bit for masonry, why you clamp before you cut, what to check before touching a light fitting. Every scenario should carry its safety step. For mains electrics or gas, the right answer is often "call a licensed professional", and a good plan says so.

Art: technique and seeing

Quizzes about art history will not improve a drawing. Questions about values, colour mixing, edges, and composition, asked as described scenarios, will. "The shadow side of the apple looks flat. What would you change first?" Pair each short quiz with a short exercise: fifteen minutes, one constraint.

Game development: build scenarios

Learn the engine by debugging small, real problems. "The player falls through the floor at high speed. Which fix?" "Input feels late. Where do you read it?" Name the engine (Godot, Unity, Unreal) so the answers use its actual concepts, and make the first project tiny enough to finish.

Coding: read, predict, fix

Reading code is most of the job, so most of the practice should be reading code. Short snippets: what does this print, where is the bug, which change fixes it. Definitions come for free once you can predict behaviour.

What stays the same for every skill

  • One next step a day. The plan decides; you start.
  • Mistakes come back. What you missed returns a few days later, then a week later.
  • Plans get corrected. If a module is vague or off target, the plan should change. Tell your tutor, and a good one fixes it.

Questions people ask

Can an app teach a physical skill like lifting or woodworking?

It cannot replace doing it, or a coach watching you. It can teach the decisions, plan your practice, and bring back what you got wrong.

What should a quiz look like for a creative skill?

Scenarios and choices, not definitions: a described painting that looks muddy and which fix to try, a chord progression and what comes next.
